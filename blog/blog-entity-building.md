# Why Your Logo on GitHub Is Worth More Than a Backlink (And How to Build a Brand AI Systems Trust)

**Category:** SEO & GEO Strategy  
**Reading time:** 12 minutes  
**Skill level:** Beginner — no technical knowledge required

---

If you've been chasing backlinks to rank higher on Google, you're playing an older game.

The businesses winning search in 2025 — and increasingly winning recommendations from AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews — aren't just building links. They're building something more fundamental: **proof that they exist**.

This is called **entity building**. And once you understand it, you'll never think about SEO the same way again.

---

## What Is a "Brand Entity" and Why Does It Matter?

Google and AI systems don't just count links anymore. They build a picture — a digital fingerprint — of every business on the internet.

Think of it like this: imagine you're trying to verify that a company is real and trustworthy. You'd probably:

- Search for their website
- Look them up on LinkedIn
- Check if they appear on Google Maps
- See if other trusted sources mention them
- Look for consistent branding across platforms

Google and AI do exactly the same thing. Automatically. Constantly.

When enough trusted sources agree that **Civados** is a real, legitimate business that provides AI automation services — Google promotes you. When ChatGPT or Perplexity gets asked *"who provides AI services for local businesses?"* — they recommend you.

This cross-referencing process is called **entity consolidation**, and it's the single most powerful thing you can do for both traditional SEO and the new wave of AI-driven search (known as GEO — Generative Engine Optimisation).

---

## The Difference Between Backlinks and Entity Signals

| Backlinks (Old Game) | Entity Signals (New Game) |
|---|---|
| One website linking to yours | Multiple trusted platforms confirming your existence |
| Quantity matters | Consistency and authority matter |
| Can be gamed | Much harder to fake |
| Affects Google rankings | Affects Google rankings AND AI recommendations |
| Diminishing returns | Compounding returns |

Here's the key insight: **every place your brand assets live on an authoritative platform is a vote of confidence** — not just a link, but a signal that says *"this business is real, established, and consistent."*

That's why uploading your logo to GitHub is worth more than most people think.

## Why Your Logo URL Matters (The Technical Bit, Made Simple)

When you host your logo on a high-authority platform and reference it from your website, you're doing two things:

1. **Creating a consistent brand signal** — the same logo, name, and description appearing across multiple trusted platforms tells Google these are all the same entity
2. **Building a citation trail** — AI systems are trained on data from across the web; the more your brand appears in high-quality, consistent contexts, the more likely it is to be included in AI recommendations

The platforms that carry the most weight, and why:

### GitHub
**Domain Authority: Very High | AI Training Data: Yes**

GitHub is one of the most crawled sites on the internet. It's used heavily in AI training datasets. A public repository with your brand name, logo, description, and a link back to your website is a legitimate, indexed citation that AI systems recognise.

We recently published the [Civados AI Resource Library on GitHub](https://github.com/SavechM/civados-local-business-ai-resources) — a free resource for local businesses. The `/assets/logos/` folder now serves as a canonical, publicly accessible location for our brand assets.

### LinkedIn Company Page
**Domain Authority: Extremely High | AI Training Data: Yes**

LinkedIn is one of the first places AI systems look when verifying business legitimacy. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude all pull LinkedIn data heavily. A complete company page with consistent branding directly influences whether AI recommends you.

### Google Business Profile
**Domain Authority: Google | AI Training Data: Google's Own**

If Google owns the data, Google trusts it absolutely. A fully completed Google Business Profile is the single highest-trust entity signal for local businesses. Google's own AI uses this data when answering "who is the best [service] near me?"

### Crunchbase
**Domain Authority: High | AI Training Data: Yes**

Perplexity and ChatGPT cite Crunchbase constantly. When someone asks an AI tool *"who is [company]?"* — Crunchbase listings frequently appear in the answer. A free listing takes 10 minutes.

### Google Sites
**Domain Authority: Google | AI Training Data: Yes**

A simple brand page on Google Sites (sites.google.com/view/civados) carries implicit trust because Google owns the domain. It's free, takes 20 minutes to create, and acts as a corroborating source for your brand identity.

## Step-by-Step Tutorial: Build Your Brand Entity From Scratch

This tutorial assumes you have a website and a logo. Everything below is free.

**Time required:** 2-3 hours total  
**Technical skill needed:** None

---

### Step 1 — GitHub Brand Repository (30 minutes)

**What you're doing:** Creating a public resource that establishes your brand on one of the most authoritative platforms on the internet.

**What to create:**
- A public repository named `[your-brand]-resources` or `[your-brand]-ai-resources`
- An `/assets/logos/` folder containing your logo files
- A detailed README that describes your business, services, and links back to your website

**Your logo URL will then look like:**
```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/[username]/[repo-name]/main/assets/logos/your-logo.png
```

This URL is:
- Permanently accessible
- Indexed by Google
- Crawled by AI training systems
- A legitimate citation on a high-authority domain

**Pro tip:** Include your business name, location, services, and website URL in the README. This text gets indexed and helps Google understand what your business does.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Brand presence on GitHub with consistent name, logo, and description linking to your website.

---

### Step 2 — Google Business Profile (20 minutes)

**What you're doing:** Claiming the single most trusted local business listing on the internet.

1. Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com)
2. Click **Manage now**
3. Search for your business name — if it doesn't appear, click **Add your business**
4. Fill in every field:
   - Business name (exactly as it appears everywhere else)
   - Category (choose the most specific one available)
   - Address or service area
   - Phone number
   - Website URL
   - Business hours
   - Description (use your exact brand description — consistency matters)
5. Upload your logo and cover photo
6. Verify via postcard, phone, or email

**Critical:** Use your business name, description, and logo exactly as they appear on your website and GitHub. Consistency across platforms is the signal.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Google's own database confirms your business exists, where it operates, and what it does.

---

### Step 3 — LinkedIn Company Page (20 minutes)

**What you're doing:** Establishing your brand on the world's most trusted professional network — heavily used by AI systems for business verification.

1. Go to [linkedin.com/company/setup/new](https://linkedin.com/company/setup/new)
2. Fill in:
   - Company name (exact match to your website and Google Business Profile)
   - LinkedIn URL: choose `linkedin.com/company/[your-brand-name]`
   - Website: your main domain
   - Industry: choose the most relevant option
   - Company size
   - Type: Privately Held
3. Write your About section — use your brand description, include your key services and location
4. Upload your logo (profile image) and a cover image
5. Add your founding date, specialities, and any other fields available
6. Publish

**Pro tip:** In the Specialities field, list your exact service keywords. LinkedIn indexes these and they contribute to AI entity recognition.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Brand presence on LinkedIn with consistent name, logo, description, website link, and professional context.

---

### Step 4 — Crunchbase Free Listing (15 minutes)

**What you're doing:** Getting listed on the database AI tools cite most often when answering questions about companies.

1. Go to [crunchbase.com](https://crunchbase.com) and create a free account
2. Click **Add a company**
3. Fill in:
   - Legal name (exact match)
   - Short description (use the same description you used on LinkedIn and Google)
   - Website
   - Founded date
   - Headquarters location
   - Categories (choose 2-3 that fit your business)
   - Number of employees
4. Add your logo
5. Save and submit for review

Crunchbase reviews new listings before publishing — typically within a few days.

**Why this matters for AI:** When someone asks Perplexity or ChatGPT about your company, Crunchbase is one of the first databases they query. A listing here dramatically increases the chance you're mentioned.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Brand listed in a business database used heavily by AI systems for company verification and recommendation.

---

### Step 5 — Google Sites Brand Page (20 minutes)

**What you're doing:** Creating a brand page on a Google-owned domain, which Google's algorithm treats with implicit trust.

1. Go to [sites.google.com](https://sites.google.com)
2. Click **+** to create a new site
3. Name it your brand name
4. Set the site URL to: `sites.google.com/view/[your-brand-name]`
5. Build a simple one-page brand profile:
   - Your logo (top of page)
   - Brand name and tagline
   - 2-3 paragraph description of what you do and who you serve
   - Your services (listed clearly)
   - Your location
   - Link to your main website
   - Link to your LinkedIn company page
   - Link to your GitHub
6. Publish

Keep it simple. The value is in the consistent brand signal on a Google-owned domain — not in the design.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Brand profile on Google's own platform, creating a direct connection between your brand identity and Google's entity database.

---

### Step 6 — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC (Technical — One-Time Setup)

**What you're doing:** Proving to email systems that your domain is legitimate — which indirectly contributes to domain trust and entity verification.

These are three DNS records that tell the internet your email is authorised, signed, and protected. Without them, your emails may land in spam and your domain appears less established.

- **SPF** — says which servers are allowed to send email from your domain
- **DKIM** — digitally signs your outgoing emails to prove they came from you
- **DMARC** — tells receiving mail servers what to do with emails that fail the above checks

If you're using Google Workspace for email, these records are set up in your domain's DNS (Cloudflare, for example) and take about 20 minutes total.

**For DMARC reporting:** Use a free service like [Postmark DMARC](https://dmarc.postmarkapp.com) so you get readable reports without exposing an internal email address publicly in your DNS records.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Domain is verified as legitimate by email authentication standards — contributes to overall domain trust.

---

### Step 7 — Press Release (Optional — When You Have News)

**What you're doing:** Creating a citation trail across news and PR platforms that AI systems index.

You don't need major coverage. A single press release on a free/low-cost wire service creates:
- Indexed mentions of your brand on authoritative news domains
- A citation trail that AI systems recognise as a signal of legitimacy
- Backlinks from news-adjacent domains

**Free/low-cost options:**
- [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com) — from $49 per release
- [PR.com](https://www.pr.com) — free basic listing
- [OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com) — free

**When to do this:** A new service launch, a business milestone, a partnership, or a significant piece of content (like a free resource library). Not worth doing without genuine news.

**Entity signal created:** ✅ Brand mentioned on news and PR platforms — contributes to AI citation likelihood.

## The Consistency Rule — The Most Important Thing in This Article

None of the above works if your brand information is inconsistent across platforms.

Google and AI systems are looking for **corroboration** — multiple independent sources saying the same thing about you. If your business name is "Civados" on your website, "Civados Ltd" on LinkedIn, and "CIVADOS" on Crunchbase, these look like different entities and the signals don't consolidate.

**Before you start, lock in these five things and use them identically everywhere:**

1. **Business name** — exact capitalisation, no variations
2. **Tagline / one-line description** — same wording across all platforms
3. **Logo** — same primary logo file, same dimensions where possible
4. **Website URL** — always `https://` with or without `www` — pick one and stick to it
5. **Business category** — choose the most accurate one and use it consistently

## How to Check If It's Working

Once you've completed the steps above, test your entity presence:

**Test 1 — Google Knowledge Panel**
Search for your business name on Google. If Google has consolidated your entity, you'll see a Knowledge Panel on the right side of the results with your logo, description, and links to your profiles. This can take 4-12 weeks to appear after completing the steps above.

**Test 2 — AI Recommendation Test**
Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity and ask:
- *"Who provides AI automation services for local businesses?"*
- *"Tell me about [your business name]"*
- *"What companies help local businesses with GoHighLevel?"*

Note what comes back. Repeat every 4-6 weeks. As your entity signals consolidate, your likelihood of appearing in AI answers increases.

**Test 3 — Brand Search**
Search `"[your business name]"` in quotes on Google. Count how many distinct authoritative sources appear. The more the better.
