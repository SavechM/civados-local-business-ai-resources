# AI Prompts: Reputation & Review Building

> Your online reputation is your most valuable local business asset. These prompts help you systematically build it.

---

## Prompt 1 — SMS Review Request (Post-Job)

```
Write a short SMS to request a Google review from a customer who just had a job completed.

Business name: [NAME]
Customer name: [FIRST NAME]
Service completed: [SERVICE]
Google review link: [YOUR LINK]

The SMS should:
- Be under 160 characters
- Feel personal, not automated
- Include the review link
- Not beg or pressure them

Example output to aim for:
"Hi Sarah, thanks for choosing [Business] today! If you have 60 seconds, we'd love a Google review: [link]. Really appreciate it!"
```

---

## Prompt 2 — In-Person Review Request Script

```
Write a script for a staff member or business owner to use when asking for a review in person or on the phone, just after completing a job.

Business type: [TYPE]
Tone: [natural / professional / casual]

The script should:
- Come after a genuine "how was everything?" check-in
- Not feel like a corporate script
- Make it easy for the customer to say yes
- Mention how quick it is (60 seconds)
- Include a fall-back if they don't have time right now ("No worries — I'll send you a link")

Keep it under 5 sentences.
```

## Prompt 3 — Reputation Building Email Campaign (3-Part)

```
Write a 3-email sequence designed to build Google reviews from existing happy customers.

Business name: [NAME]
Business type: [TYPE]
Google review link: [LINK]
Estimated customer database size: [NUMBER]

Email 1 (Ask): Direct review request — warm, genuine, explains why it matters to a small business
Email 2 (3 days later, no action): Follow-up — softer ask, different angle
Email 3 (7 days later, no action): Final message — thank them regardless, leave door open

For each email:
- Subject line (3 options)
- Body copy (under 150 words each)
- CTA

Tone: Like a message from a local business owner, not a corporation.
```

## Prompt 4 — Responding to "I'll Leave a Review" (But They Haven't)

```
Write a gentle follow-up SMS or email for a customer who said they'd leave a review but hasn't after 5 days.

Business name: [NAME]
Customer name: [FIRST NAME]
Google review link: [LINK]

The message should:
- Not be passive aggressive or guilt-tripping
- Assume they just forgot (because they probably did)
- Make it easy by including the direct link
- Be under 100 words
```

## Reputation Strategy for Local Businesses

**The only three things that matter for your Google rating:**

1. **Volume** — more reviews = more trust signals to Google and AI
2. **Recency** — reviews older than 6 months count for less
3. **Responses** — replying to reviews signals you're an active, engaged business

**Benchmarks to aim for:**

| Business Size | Target Reviews | Target Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Solo operator | 25+ | 4.7+ |
| Small team (2-5) | 75+ | 4.8+ |
| Established (5-10) | 150+ | 4.8+ |
| Multi-location | 50+ per location | 4.7+ |

**Review velocity matters:** 5 reviews in one week looks suspicious. 2-3 per week consistently for 3 months is ideal.

**Never:**
- Buy fake reviews
- Offer incentives for reviews (against Google's terms)
- Ask in bulk all at once after years of none

---

## The GEO Impact of Reviews

When AI tools like ChatGPT or Perplexity recommend local businesses, they draw on:

- Google Business Profile data
- Review sentiment and volume
- Response rate and recency
- Mentions across the web

A business with 200 reviews at 4.9 stars with regular responses is far more likely to be cited by AI systems as a trusted option than one with 12 reviews and no responses.

Your review strategy is not just about Google rankings. It's about being the business AI recommends.
